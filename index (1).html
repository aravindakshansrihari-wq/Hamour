A taste from a sample issue

Why a share price can dip overnight with nothing going wrong.

(Spoiler: it’s a date, not a drama.)

When a company pays a dividend, it sets a cut-off called the ex-dividend date. Own the share before it and the payout is yours. Buy on or after it and the payout goes to the seller.

Emaar Properties just did exactly this. Its one-off AED 0.50 dividend went ex-dividend on Friday 13 November, so shares bought from that day don’t get it. It’s paid on 27 November.

So on an ex-dividend morning, the price usually opens lower by roughly the dividend. Nothing broke. The value just moved from the share price into shareholders’ pockets.

Meanwhile, in the same family

Emaar Properties is offering AED 15 a share in cash to take Emaar Development private.

It already owns about 80% of Emaar Development, so the offer is for the rest. AED 15 is 19.4% above the 2 November close of AED 12.56, the last trading day before the plan was announced. The offer runs until 10 December, subject to conditions.

Taking a company private means buying out the other shareholders so its shares stop trading on the exchange. If Emaar ends up with 90% plus one share, it can buy out the holders who didn’t accept, at the same AED 15.
